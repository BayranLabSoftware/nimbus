# Porta 1 — what becomes of the settled cloud mass (rules 1232 and 1233)

Item (b) of rule 1224 asks two things: what the published fragment-cloud work does with a cloud once
it has slowed, and what a source independent of this project's cases says of a debris cloud's late
fate — vapour, dust, pieces. The first was answered in rule 1227 (R17, W18 and Wheeler et al.'s
poster: the model stops a slowed cloud and sets no later fate for it; cloud mass is never meteorite
mass). This is the second, declared before any search (rule 1232) and read from open copies only,
every altitude masked.

## Fact sheets (rule 1192's form)

**Della Corte et al. (2013).** A balloon collector (DUSTER) flew through the debris cloud of an
unidentified carbonaceous bolide over the Arctic in June 2008 and caught intact and thermally eroded
micrometre grains, melt, and vapour condensates — CaO and carbon nanoparticles. _Form:_ none
quantitative; the settled debris of a bolide is dust aloft, micrometre and nanometre sized. _Domain:_
one event, one collection. _Free:_ every share and every size; the residence times are, in the
authors' words, as yet unknown. _Discriminating observable:_ in-situ collection after a known
bolide. _Refuted by:_ no such products where a known bolide's cloud was sampled.

**Schulz & Glassmeier (2020).** A study of the yearly mass budget, not an entry model. It cites three
dust clouds against the entry mass — Chelyabinsk about 24 %, the Antarctic bolide of 3 September
2004 about 79 % (at least 47 %, from Klekociuk et al.), 2008 TC3 about 20 % (at least 15 %) — and
assumes an aerosol share that rises linearly with the logarithm of the mass, to 50 % at 10⁷ kg; the
dust sediments within several months. _Form:_ that linear law, an assumption by the authors' own
account. _Domain:_ 10⁻² to 10⁸ kg. _Free:_ the form itself, fit on three events of which two are this
project's development cases. _Discriminating observable:_ a dust cloud's mass after a bolide, against
its entry mass. _Refuted by:_ large bolides whose dust clouds carry little of their mass.

## Verdict (rule 1233 (d))

- **The kind of fate is identifiable**, and needs no new constant: what a large bolide neither
  vaporises nor lands stays aloft as micrometre and smaller dust that sediments over months. It is
  never ground mass or meteorite mass, and once slowed it deposits no energy of consequence — which
  is what the branch's settle stop already does with it.
- **The share is not identifiable** from these sources: no form independent of the development cases
  fixes it. The one independent measurement (47 to 79 % of the 2004 bolide's mass) is read here only
  through a review; its primary, Klekociuk et al. (2005), waits for Andrea's download, and the verdict
  on the share will be written again when it is read.
- Nothing is coded, fitted or adopted. A known bolide's dust fraction is an observable the branch
  could later be held to, never a constant to tune it on.
