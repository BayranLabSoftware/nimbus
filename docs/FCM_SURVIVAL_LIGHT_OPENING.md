## The question (rule 1178)

Two findings, treated as one because they may share a cause:

- **Survival**: every development case lands far more mass than is known — Chelyabinsk 24 to 453 t
  against Popova et al.'s 4 to 6 t; Tagish Lake 6.7 to 10 t against W18's 190 kg; 2008 TC3 7.0 to
  10.4 t against 283 g; Tunguska up to 911 t where nothing was found. The registered tuning of rule
  1160 cut it without reaching the objective.
- **The deposit–light mismatch**: round 3's diagnostic found the branch's main peak of energy
  deposition close to the onset of fragmentation, at or above the brightest flare's altitude. The
  same pattern holds on the development cases themselves: the peak sits 0.5 to 3.3 km below the
  first break on sixteen of eighteen of them.

The question is which physical process or representation is missing — not which interval of
parameters brings the worst cases inside their targets. No prior is redrawn to answer it (rule 1181).

No hypothesis is assumed true, and a built test accompanies whichever is coded (rule 1182).

## The prohibition (rule 1181)

No luminous efficiency, ablation coefficient or other parameter is chosen, narrowed or tuned to move
a peak or a landed mass toward an observed value. Every number is cited from a source read before the
rule, with its place; where a source gives a range, the reading is fixed before any comparison.

## The package (rule 1183)

At the end of H1 and H3: the synthetic light curves and the mass decomposition for every development
case, plotted against the mechanical deposit and, where read, the source's heights; whether H1 is
supported and by how much; where the excess mass sits; a decision, written before H2 is coded, on
whether to proceed and in what declared form.

## The fifth set's custody protocol (rule 1184) and its reconnaissance (rule 1185)

The reviewer's five steps, superseding rule 1158's for any future download: Andrea deposits the file
in a custody folder outside the repository; the analyst reads only its hash, byte size, public
